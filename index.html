Practical

Annoying to discover late.

Ship the festival gear. Takkyubin from Minakami on the 12th to the Tokyo hotel for the 20th — all four are back there that night — or to Eri's. Four days of planes and ferries follow, and a tent has no business on the Jomon Sugi trail.
Rail pass — look again. Kyoto → Tokyo pushes the 16th–20th to roughly ¥61,000 of shinkansen per person, against ¥50,000 for a 7-day national pass, so it now pays for everyone but Jonas — he skips Kyoto and stays under. The pass still excludes Nozomi and Mizuho: Hikari and Sakura instead, maybe 30 min slower on the Tokaido run. Regional Kyushu / Sanyo passes worth a check.
Vegetarian, in practice. Dashi (bonito stock) is in most broths, miso soup, simmered vegetables and many dipping sauces, so plant-looking food often is not. Tokyo has range, and Jonas has five nights there. Yakushima and Kyushu are the thin part: Fukuoka is a pork town, the yatai a write-off, and the Jomon Sugi day needs food carried in.
Cash on Yakushima. Cards work everywhere else. 7-Eleven ATMs take foreign cards; mobile Suica in Apple Wallet handles transit.
Split Jonas' two Tokyo nights. Anywhere on the 19th, Kamata or a Haneda hotel on the 20th — ten minutes from the terminal. The other three arrive that afternoon and can stay central.

Fixed anchors: Labyrinth Oct 10–12 (Minakami Hodaigi), the Haneda–Kagoshima–Yakushima flights Oct 12, Jonas' 07:50 departure Oct 21.
Where it splits: Oct 17. Fukuoka → Okayama is ~1 h 45. Hiroshima and Itsukushima are an optional detour on that line — leave Fukuoka around 13:00 and you still reach Okayama by 18:30. Same bed either way.
Where it splits again: Oct 19. Jonas goes Okayama → Tokyo, ~3 h 15, skipping Kansai; the rest go Okayama → Kyoto, ~1 h. It closes the next day — Kyoto → Tokyo is ~2 h 20, so all four sleep in Tokyo on the last night.
Watch: Yakushima weather (typhoon tail) can cancel the Oct 15 ferry; Oct 14 is the buffer. Naoshima museums shut Mondays and want advance tickets.
